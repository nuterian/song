One command

It renders the video.

Three lines at the bottom left, each word lighting up as it lands and the stack rising when the line turns over. Everything behind them is generated from the track itself — the section picks the palette, the light answers the mix, one line traces the amplitude. No footage, no template, nothing to supply.

Why there is no frame renderer in here

Because ASS subtitles have had karaoke tags since the VirtualDub era. \kf sweeps a fill across a run of text over a given duration, and this project already knows where every word starts and ends. So the lyrics are a format conversion and a burn-in, and shaping, wrapping and antialiasing come from libass rather than from code written here to arrive somewhere worse.

The arithmetic that matters

ASS counts in centiseconds. Round each word on its own and you lose half a centisecond a time — 13 across a 100-word line, all of it landing at the end, which is the half nobody checks. Every duration here is the difference between two absolute positions instead.

Sensitive, subtle and smooth are three different things

Sensitive is timing: the frame's brightness tracks the mix with its best correlation at zero lag. Subtle is amount: the whole frame moves through 6.9% of its range over ten seconds. Smooth is frequency — and that one cost a round to learn. The first version was sensitive and subtle and flickered, because a kick swings a field 12 of 255 in three frames and a large area doing that is read as a twitch, however smooth the curve driving it. The fields average over 0.30 s now; the waveform keeps the unsmoothed copy, because a thin stroke can show detail a field cannot.

Everything eases, including the part that cannot

A line does not slide between stops, it eases — but \move is linear and has no easing of its own. So a slot change is cut into three segments whose lengths follow a smoothstep, and because each segment is its own event carrying its own share of the fade and the scale, everything about a line eases on one curve.

The premise

You already know the words.

Transcribe-then-match falls apart on songs: a chorus that repeats four times gives four identical transcripts and nothing says which is which. Since the lyrics are known, this is forced alignment — the text is consumed in order, so repeats resolve by position for free.

How three models cross-examine each other

One aligner is not enough, and an AI-generated song has no reference timing to check against. So three independent passes disagree in public.

1 · Demucs isolates the vocal

Aligning against the stem rather than the mix is the single biggest accuracy win on dense productions — the aligners stop locking onto kick drums.

2 · wav2vec2 CTC anchors the structure

A Whisper pass over a whole song desynchronizes: a 4-bar instrumental outruns its attention window and the rest slides. Measured here, that put the closing line at 2:37 of a 4:46 song. CTC finds one globally optimal path, so instrumentals are absorbed as blanks.

3 · Whisper refines inside each section

With structure anchored, each section is cropped to ~20 seconds and re-aligned, where Whisper is both accurate and precise.

4 · A blind transcription adjudicates

Both aligners are told the lyrics, so both can be confidently wrong in the same place. A third pass is told nothing and reports what it hears. It is the strongest single signal in the merge — and it is what finds the chorus repeat somebody forgot to type.

Accuracy you can check

Every line is scored.

There is no ground truth, so accuracy is estimated from evidence: six signals per line, rolled into a number. Review goes where the benchmark says it should, and lines that are fine carry no mark at all.

Proof the score discriminates

A quality score that always says "fine" is worthless, so it was run against a deliberately degraded alignment — the same track aligned against the full mix, skipping vocal isolation. Mean line score falls from 94.5 to 69.9, vocal coverage from 99% to 9%, and lines needing review go from 2 of 33 to 11.

Read the flags as "look here", not "this is wrong." On this track both flagged lines are correctly placed — they are flagged because two model families disagree about them by seconds, which is the flags doing their job.

Where models disagree

You settle it by ear, in five seconds.

Of 177 words, two aligners agreed on 137 — you never see those. The rest play you both candidates from the moment each claims the word begins. If neither is right, drag a third onto the waveform.

What is deliberately not automated

Snapping every word start to its nearest detected onset. It looks like free accuracy, but on this track 41% of word starts have more than one onset within ±150 ms — snapping would be a coin flip wearing a lab coat. Where two independent models disagree and both readings are plausible, no heuristic settles it honestly, so a human ear does.

Two commands.

Roughly five minutes to align a five-minute track, and thirteen more to render it at 1080p. Models download once; re-runs reuse the cached stem. 

./setup.sh
./.venv/bin/python -m song
Try it in your browser GitHub
Every command, and what lands on disk 
python -m song                          # open the app
python -m song track.wav lyrics.txt     # align, then open it
python -m song align track.wav lyrics.txt
python -m song audit workdir/my-track   # repair + list what needs an ear
python -m song score workdir/my-track   # re-run the benchmark on your edits
python -m song export workdir/my-track
python -m song video workdir/my-track   # render karaoke.mp4

Everything lands in workdir/<track>/: lyrics.lrc, lyrics.word.lrc (per-word), lyrics.srt and .vtt, plus project.json — word timings, per-line scores and the scorecard. song video adds lyrics.ass and karaoke.mp4, and caches the beat grid and a two-band split as beats.json.

Lyrics input is plain text: one line per lyric line, a blank line between sections, and a header naming each. [Verse 1], Chorus:, (Bridge) and Verse 1 (8 bars, pulsing bass) all parse.

Demucs on Apple MPS is broken under torch 2.5 and Whisper hits unimplemented sparse ops there, so it is CPU throughout.
